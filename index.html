Grounded in Practical Measurement & Embedded Logic

How an Electronics & Instrumentation Engineering background bridges physical physical measurement with intelligent digital systems.

"Applying principles of precision instrumentation, microcontroller firmware, and signal processing to solve real-world industrial monitoring and equipment reliability problems."

As a final-year student in Electronics and Instrumentation Engineering (EIE) at VNR VJIET, my academic training centers on understanding physical phenomena through sensors, conditioning noisy analog signals, and translating them into actionable digital data.

My technical focus spans industrial instrumentation and measurement, sensor interfacing and real-time data acquisition (DAQ), low-level firmware in C and Embedded C, and mathematical signal processing including digital filtering and FFT.

I am particularly driven by applying engineering methods to real-world operational challenges—from developing edge-based atrial fibrillation classifiers on STM32 microcontrollers to formulating physics-constrained message-passing graph neural networks for distributed sensor fault detection.

Hybrid Statistical–Physics Informed Graph Neural Network (SP-GNN)

Developed an unsupervised SP-GNN framework for sensor fault detection using the Intel Berkeley Research Lab (IBRL) dataset comprising 54 sensors and approximately 2.3 million readings. Integrated physics-informed message passing, graph-Laplacian regularization, and Monte-Carlo Dropout for uncertainty-aware anomaly detection.
